The invariant this design now holds to

Nothing the robot cannot measure reaches the actor. The privileged tail is the critic's input and the velocity estimator's supervised target, with a stop_gradient between the estimator and the actor, and the exporter slices the observation statistics to the actor width so no privileged number can even reach a bundle. That part was always sound. What was not sound, and is what §00 above records, is privileged information arriving through channels that are not privileged tensors: a clock pulled by foot contact, and a task input computed from ground-truth position. Reading the code is how you miss that, so it is now a test — walk_v2/tools/audit_actor_obs.py perturbs contact and absolute position and requires the actor slice to be bit-identical. It fails on every v2 checkpoint. That is the point.

What the robot actually senses

Joint encoders (with an origin that is re-randomised every power cycle), motor current, and one ICM-20948 IMU on a Sense HAT under the base. Velocity is differentiated position, not measured; torque is inferred from a datasheet Kt derated by a measured 0.85 gearbox efficiency, which is roughly twice the torque noise the policy trained against; roll and pitch are gravity-referenced and absolute while yaw is pure gyro integration and drifts. There is no foot contact sensor, no absolute position, and no measured velocity. See robot/IMU.md for the noise figures and the sim2real model.

§00b

What actually produced the gait — and the stop

The staged pipeline in §09–§11 (fixed-point solver, library solve, Raibert prior, stabilizer training, closed-loop search, feedforward absorption) was not used. Two things were:

PPO end-to-end on the policy spec source produced the locomotion. The best runner covers the full 100 m in 16/16 greedy episodes at 3.23–3.29 m/s. It is warm-started, and that matters more than it looks: 4/4 cold free-plant seeds park on the slow clock rail by 6–26 M steps and never leave, so the planar stage exists to make 4 Hz discoverable, not to transfer balance.

A cross-entropy search over gait specs produced everything PPO refused to find. It runs the policy for its per-tick residual but overrides the latched spec, so it asks "does the action space contain this?" rather than "can PPO find it?" — a distinction worth several hundred million steps. It found a stop, and it found a gait at every commanded speed from 0.0 to 3.5 m/s, all upright, on a policy that only ever produced one speed.

The stop, and why ten reward variants failed

This robot would not learn to stop. Ten measured interventions — a bigger tracking weight, a continuous command instead of a binary flag, a gentler deceleration, Laplace instead of Gaussian tracking, a capture-step term, the measured braking direction injected as a prior — all produced the same signature: it never slowed, and it fell accelerating. The cause is not the reward.

The stop command is itself the disturbance. Hold the run flag through the finish line and the identical control — no braking at all — goes from 3/512 upright to 512/512. The robot was never failing at braking; it was failing at being told to stop, because the command arrives on an input the gait is conditioned on, and the policy had spent its whole life with that input pinned. Softening it (a ramp instead of a flip) cannot help, because the problem is the channel, not its sharpness.

So the stop is not a learned behaviour. It is twelve numbers — four channels × three knots, piecewise linear over a fixed window — fitted per checkpoint by the same CEM, applied by overriding the latched spec while the policy keeps supplying its residual, and the policy is never told the run has ended. On held-out episodes with the trigger arriving at a random moment: 500/512 upright, 213/512 reaching a full standstill, median 19.6 m to stop. Three properties decide whether a schedule reproduces, all measured: it is duration-specific (replaying a 12 s fit over 16 s gives 0/512), state-specific, and checkpoint-specific, because it rides on that policy's residual.

A method note that cost three experiments. Asking the search only to avoid falling produces schedules that stay upright and never quite stop. Adding the thing actually wanted — stop, and stop short — improved robustness and distance and the stop rate simultaneously (78%→98% upright, 25%→42% stopped, 24.1 m→19.6 m). Optimising for the absence of failure gave a worse controller than optimising for the presence of success. Pushing the same search harder then lost on held-out episodes three separate times: more episodes per candidate, a heavier distance weight, more iterations. Moderate settings generalise; aggressive ones fit the episodes they were scored on.

v3: the joystick

The command channel is now the operator's, not the task's. task[0] = v_cmd / v_max — a fraction of top speed, so half a stick asks for half — drawn per episode and redrawn on a timer throughout training, with a share of draws at exactly zero. A zero command means step in place, not stand still: this plant has no passive stance to hold, it topples in 0.7–1.0 s with no gait, and the CEM confirmed a spec that holds 0.02 m/s upright. Episodes also start the way an operator actually hands the robot over — dropped from 5–10 cm above a tilt-dependent touching height, or held misaligned and released at up to ±20° of pitch — because the measured bring-up envelope today is ±5° with both feet flat, and that is a training gap, not a fact about the machine.

task[1] is reserved, and it is held at 1 — not 0. Zero is not a neutral filler on a channel that already means something. Under v2 task[1] is the distance-to-go ramp: 1 whenever the finish line is far away, 0 only to demand a stop. Pinning the reserved channel at 0 told a warm-started runner to brake on every tick — measured 2026-09-11, the 3.27 m/s runner made ~0.1 m/s under a 3.2 m/s command and fell 100% of the time inside 86 ticks, where the same checkpoint runs 600/600 upright under the sprint preset. The deployed control law must send the same 1, or the robot runs a different controller than the one that was trained.

The same warm start exposes a second trap, in the normalisation rather than the value. task[0] has variance 6.5 × 10−5 in the v2 runner — it was effectively the constant 1.0 for all of training, because the run flag only drops at the line. Obs normalisation clips at ±10σ, so with those statistics taken verbatim a 3.2 m/s command reads as −13.6σ and every command below ~0.92 clips to exactly −10: the policy cannot distinguish "stop" from "3.2 m/s", and episode length is identical (130–132 ticks) at every command. warmstart_var_floor (10−2) is what makes the channel legible again, lifting the std to 0.1 so 0.89 reads as −1.1σ. Any diagnostic that loads the checkpoint without that floor is measuring a condition no run ever sees.

What the joystick pays

Replacing the dash's speed income with a bounded tracking term is what made the first two v3 seeds rail the gait clock to its 1.5 Hz floor and fall early. Measured on the same checkpoint and plant: the sprint objective earns income 5.11/tick against 2.69 of cost and survives 600/600 ticks, while a ceiling-3.0 tracking income earned 0.57 against an almost identical 2.50. Costs were never the problem. With fall_penalty 100 and reward_dt_scale 0.5, break-even against a 3000-tick episode is ~200 ticks, so dying at once was worth ~15× living — and the optimiser found exactly that.

income = (w_track + w_fwd_speed · clip(v_cmd, 0, v_ceiling)) · exp(−|vx − v_cmd| / σ)    w_track 3.0 · w_fwd_speed 2.0 · v_ceiling 3.0 · σ 0.6 m/s
plus a constant w_alive = 1.5, which is the floor under survival, not a standing attractor: at a 3.2 m/s command standing pays 0.5/tick against running's 3.8

The income scales with what was asked, not with what was achieved. The Laplace shape still peaks only at the commanded speed — overspeeding pays less, which a bare speed income does not give — while the magnitude tracks the cost of the commanded gait, exactly as the dash's income tracked the cost of sprinting. The w_track base survives at v_cmd = 0, so holding station pays and drifting does not; a purely proportional income would have zeroed that out. Laplace and not Gaussian because a Gaussian pays 0.006 at the ~1.8 m/s error this lineage actually sits at, i.e. it is flat exactly where the policy lives.

§01

Signal path

One loop, one tick = 10 ms. The single structural change vs the current stack is the latch register between the policy and the gait generator: the policy still emits the full spec every tick (CPG-RL style), but only the value present at the phase wrap ever reaches the generator. Mid-cycle rewrites are not penalized — they are discarded, so there is no gate to park the phase against (the m3 exploit evaded the phase-gated penalty 89% of the time; a latch cannot be evaded).

Credit assignment under the latch NEW RISK

At 3 Hz and 100 Hz, 44 of the 50 action dims are no-ops on 32 of every 33 ticks: only the wrap-tick emission of the spec reaches the plant. PPO would still sample those dims with exploration noise and assign them an advantage on every tick — pure gradient noise on ~90% of the action, ~97% of the time. The fix is to mask the spec dims out of the log-probability on non-wrap ticks (a per-sample mask from the env into a custom Gaussian distribution, ~40 lines), so those dims neither perturb the ratio nor receive spurious gradients. The alternative — commit the cycle average of the emissions instead of the wrap-tick value — spreads credit over every tick with no masking, at the cost of the policy not being able to act on late-cycle information. Masking is the recommendation; it is the one genuinely new implementation risk the latch introduces.

How the estimator is trained

Supervised regression against ground truth — not PPO, and not its own actor-critic. The privileged tail already carries the true base velocity for the critic; the same three numbers double as a labelled target. After every PPO rollout a separate callback runs a few Adam epochs of MSE on that rollout’s own observations, and those gradients touch the estimator’s weights and nothing else.

Why detached. If PPO could backpropagate through the estimate, the actor would be free to bend the estimator toward whatever feature happened to raise return — and it would stop being a velocity estimate. Detaching leaves it a single-purpose regressor with its own optimum, which is also what makes it deployable: on the robot the same weights map measured history to a velocity the controller can trust.

Why it exists at all. The actor has no velocimeter, and velocity is only about R² 0.807 recoverable from the observation history — so without this the policy would have to re-derive it inside the policy net from a noisy signal, with no supervision saying whether it got it right. The estimator makes that step explicit, supervised, and loggable (est/vel_rmse).

Two practical notes: the estimator’s weights live inside the policy and ride every checkpoint, but the callback’s Adam moments do not survive a resume, which costs a few warm-up updates and nothing else. And the supervised pass reuses the PPO rollout buffer, so it adds no environment steps — only a little GPU time per update.

Pi budget: estimator + actor ≈ 232 k MACs/tick ≈ 0.9 ms at the measured 519 MFLOPS (OpenBLAS gemv). The measured 8–9 ms/tick at 200 Hz was mostly numpy dispatch, already cut from the deploy control law — at 100 Hz the 10 ms budget holds with margin, which is the deployment argument for 100 Hz in the first place.

§05

The latch, over one gait cycle

Example at f = 3 Hz: one cycle = 333 ms = 33 ticks. The policy's spec output is sampled every tick but only the wrap-time value commits. Compare the m3 exploit: with per-step specs and 0.5–50 Hz rails, the policy warped φ to park where the penalty gate was free (93% of the time) and rewrote the spec every 5 ms. Here the exploit is not expensive — it is unrepresentable.

Ground feedback into the clock — contact-triggered resync REMOVED in v3

This was built, measured, and then taken out. DASH-01 has no foot contact sensor, so nothing on the robot can produce the touchdown event — the runtime free-runs the clock. That made the resync a privileged input in the strict sense: it moved φ, which the actor reads as [cos, sin], and it could carry the commit flag across the wrap, so two actor inputs depended on a signal the hardware cannot supply.

Measured 2026-09-11 with κ forced to 0 on the same checkpoints: the dash is unchanged (103.1–104.9 m at 3.20–3.28 m/s free-running, against 103.1–104.8 m at 3.23–3.29 m/s with the resync) and braking is if anything better (511/512 upright held-out, against 476/512). It was buying nothing. v3 compiles the whole block out (resync_enable=False): no contact quantity reaches the clock at all. The parameters below are kept because v2 runs replay with them, and because controller_v2.note_contact() is the hook if a contact sensor ever exists — but do not build an IMU touchdown estimator for this, it is not what limits the controller. Brake-point placement dominates it by a wide margin.

Every gait above is timed by a free-running clock. Texture, camber, a gust or a scuffed swing all show up first as an early or late touchdown, and a time-clocked reference then fights each one through the residual for the rest of the cycle. The hybrid-zero-dynamics remedy is a state-based phase (stance-leg angle), which running lacks during flight; the running-appropriate form is the spring-mass one — the touchdown event resets the clock. The env keeps its own estimate of where in φ each foot normally lands and pulls the clock toward it when the foot actually lands.

§06

One series, five relationship knobs

u_L(φ) = n_L + (1+s)·A·S(φ)         + o
u_R(φ) = n_R − (1−s)·A·S(φ − π − Δ) + o

The right leg is literally the same series, mirrored by the structural minus sign, read π+Δ later, scaled by (1−s), and biased by o. The mirror defect is then a one-line identity:

u_L(φ) + u_R(φ+π)  =  A·[(1+s)·S(φ) − (1−s)·S(φ−Δ)] + 2o      = 0 for all φ  ⇔  Δ = 0, s = 0, o = 0

So knobs at zero is exactly the mirror-symmetric gait, and “penalise the difference between the left and right channel” reduces to a standing price on five numbers. Because Δ and s are global (one per leg pair) and S is shared, the two legs always have the same waveform shape: they can differ in timing, amplitude and offset, never in stance plateau — duty-cycle limp is unrepresentable, heading trim is not.

The hop, the bound, and the “half-quadruped”

All of them are corners of this knob space, not exclusions. s = ±1 zeroes one leg’s amplitude; with o_cam + o_thigh lifting it and o_hip adducting the working foot toward the midline, that is THE RUNNER’s one-legged hop. Δ = ±π puts both legs in phase: the bound, the gait a biped can borrow from a quadruped. Δ_max decides whether the bound is reachable; s already makes the hop reachable. They are priced by w_knob, not forbidden — the policy is free to test the hypothesis that one of them is optimal, on a plant that now carries every cost sprint_m3 dodged.

Those costs are why the hop lost when roll was freed (RUN 8): the moment does not go away when the foot is adducted to the midline, it moves — from a body-level topple to a joint-level load on hip_roll, whose axis sits 0.19 m off the midline. A hop that is 80% airborne must deliver ≈5× body weight per stance: ≈140 N·m on the AK60-39, against a 72 N·m peak (61.2 in the model). On the m3 rail that moment was carried by the constraint for free. The only escape is to roll the torso over the foot instead of adducting the foot under the torso — ≈11° at 4.76 Hz, roughly 50 N·m of roll torque, marginal — and the thermal budget still charges one leg for the whole gait (the hop already sat at 100% of continuous). The bound is roll-feasible and shares load across both legs, but no flight-heavy gait beats a grounded one without elastic storage (flight bound 6.2 m/s), and a rigid ankle stores nothing.

Symmetry price and equivariance

L = L_PPO + w_knob·‖k(s)‖² + w_sym·‖ k(M_o s) + k(s) ‖²      k = (Δ/Δ_max, s, o/o_max),   w_sym ≈ 0.5

The first term is the standing asymmetry price. The second says: mirror the state and the knobs must negate — a robot drifting left trims right, its mirror image trims left, both satisfy it exactly. Asymmetry justified by the state is free; asymmetry adopted for no reason is not. Only the observation mirror Mo needs deriving; on the action side the mirror is “negate the five knobs, swap the residual with the verified sign rule.”

Verified sign rule, and a correction to the current code. A pure-FK test (mj_forward at the keyframe, no dynamics) gives one uniform rule: joint pattern (+,−) is symmetric and (+,+) is antisymmetric, for both joint families — thigh (+,−) moves both feet the same way in x (dx −0.147/−0.147), hip_roll (+,−) moves them oppositely in y (dy +0.147/−0.147). One rule looks “flipped” only because the mirror preserves fore-aft and negates lateral.

This contradicts fourier_gait.py:212-213, which has the two hip_roll channels swapped: as wired, s_width is a lateral shift and the abduction reflex is a symmetric widen/narrow. Roll is antisymmetric under mirroring, so an equivariant response must enter as (+,+). Wired (+,−), the reflex structurally cannot make a directional lateral capture step; no learned gain fixes it. §09

Pipeline — gait library, stabilizer, search NEW

The single-network design of §01–§06 asks one policy to find the gait and stabilize it. The 2026-09-01 freeze study showed the split the physics wants: the stabilizing correction is small (0.018–0.035 rad RMS per joint, p99 < 0.14 rad, within-cycle), the orbit is unstable with an e-fold time of about one cycle, and gait expressibility was never the constraint. The gait-library pipeline below therefore finds the gait outside RL as a solved periodic orbit, and trains the network only for the job the freeze study proved it must do. Every component of §01–§08 is reused; what changes is who emits the spec.

Two variants, one interface. Library variant (recommended build order): the spec is a per-episode command drawn from the library box, the action is the residual, the search picks the gait. Single-network variant (§02–§04 as written): the policy's latched head emits the spec. The env, the once-block, the reward and the margin gate are identical; the fallback is one config flag if the fixed-point solver finds no usable orbits.

The residual term is what writes the plant physics into θ: the search lowers the stabilizer's bill by moving the gait toward where the leg carries its weight cheaply, which is the anisotropy, the moment balance and the loaded 4-bar geometry landing in the Fourier parameters rather than being repaired every cycle. Output: the speed–margin front; the §08 gate picks the point.

Stage 5 — feedforward absorption and the hardware loop

  • Absorb what is left. Phase-average the residual over ≥ 50 cycles, project r̄(φ) onto the weighted Fourier basis (least squares), add to θ, repeat ≤ 5 times or until RMS(r̄) < 0.01 rad. Stays inside the stabilizer's box, so nothing retrains.
  • On the robot, deploy θ* and log residual RMS/saturation and the touchdown-phase error. Residual effort far above its simulated value means the nominal is wrong for the real leg: run one absorption pass on hardware data and redeploy. The gait is tuned by θ within the box; retraining is not on the path.
  • Diagnostics that gate each stage: gait_diag.py frequency rails and residual saturation (greedy residual saturation must stay rare; sustained saturation on a legal gait means the residual has become the waveform), λ_max from Stage 0, the §08 envelope.

§13

Open decisions

  • Δ_max — is the bound testable? ±0.6 rad (the CPG arm’s value) forbids both-legs-in-phase; ±π allows it and lets the thermal budget, the hip_roll torque cap and the flight bound price it. s = ±1 already makes the one-legged hop reachable. The spirit of the design is to price, not forbid — but widening Δ_max is a deliberate choice, made once.
  • Log-prob mask on non-wrap ticks — the latch’s one new implementation risk (§04). Verify the wrap flag lines up with the env’s commit bit-for-bit before training.
  • Harmonic count for impedance — N=3 for uniformity; the profile probably needs N=2, which cuts the action to 46. Trim after the first run.
  • w_knob, w_sym and stats symmetrisation — start w_sym ≈ 0.5; if VecNormalize stats drift asymmetric early, mirror the stats rather than raise the weight.
  • Slow-clock billing — watch latched f mid-dash; amortise by f/f_ref only if it drifts to the 0.5 Hz floor.
  • 100 Hz on the real drives — bench-test before the run commits; 50 Hz measured reproducibly worse on these drives, so 100 Hz is an open empirical question.
  • λ̄, the multiplier bound in the library solve (§09) — ≤ 1 asks for self-stable orbits, which may not exist at running speed on a rigid-ankle leg; a bound around 1.5–2 per cycle is holdable by a residual with the measured correction sizes. Report λ_max on every entry first, then choose.
  • Library box width (§09 Stage 3) — too wide and most episodes are infeasible gaits with no signal; too narrow and the search returns the nominal. Start at ±10% amplitudes and widen by curriculum on survival.
  • Resync gain and window (§05) — κ 0.5 / ±0.15 cycle are priors; measure the touchdown-phase error distribution on the first stabilizer run and set W at its 99th percentile.
  • Lateral push envelope (§08) — the last axis with no data on any plant; run it on RUN 8 v2 before the stabilizer trains, so the first lateral number is a baseline and not a design guess.
